💪 8 Strength Exercises for Pickleball Players

Build functional strength to improve performance and prevent injury

Source: Experience Life

Why Strength Matters for Pickleball

Endurance and agility are obvious staple skills for anyone who plays pickleball. But as it is for many sports, strength is also integral for improving abilities and staying safe from injury.

"Due to the size of the court and speed and style of play, pickleball players require a lot of functional flexibility — and strength throughout those ranges of motion," says Joel Watts, personal training leader at Life Time in Charlotte, N.C.

"Developing strength should be a focus of players' off-court efforts in order to prevent injury and maximize performance."

💡 Recommendation: Incorporate these eight moves into your exercise routine 2-3 times per week for best results.

🔄 Rotational Strength Exercises

1 Split Stance Single-Arm Cable Chest Press With Rotation

Benefits: Increases rotational strength and speed while training core and lower-body stability.
How to: Stand in a split stance with the cable set at chest height. Press the handle forward while rotating your torso, then return to start. Keep your core engaged throughout the movement.

2 Split Stance Single-Arm Cable Row With Rotation

Benefits: Builds rotational power and helps maintain stability during dynamic movements on the court.
How to: Stand in a split stance facing the cable machine. Pull the handle toward your body while rotating your torso, then control the return. Focus on squeezing your back muscles at the end of the pull.

3 Half-Kneeling Lift

Benefits: Improves rotational strength and speed while training core and lower-body stability.
How to: Kneel on one knee with the other foot flat on the floor. Hold a weight or cable at chest height, then rotate through your torso to lift the weight overhead. Lower with control and repeat.

4 Medicine Ball Toss

Benefits: Develops explosive rotational power for powerful shots and dynamic court movements.
How to: Stand in an athletic stance holding a medicine ball. Rotate your torso and throw the ball against a wall or to a partner, catching and repeating. Focus on hip and core power generation.

🦵 Lower Body & Balance Exercises

5 Single-Leg Deadlift

Benefits: "This exercise is an excellent choice to not only strengthen the leg muscles but to also improve your balance and coordination. This exercise is vital in improving your athletic ability and speed." — Andrew Blosser
How to: Stand on one leg with a slight bend in the knee. Hinge at the hips and lower your torso while extending your free leg behind you for balance. Hold a dumbbell in one hand or both. Return to standing and repeat.

8 Goblet Squat

Benefits: "Builds a foundation of strength and mobility, which enhances your ability to explosively move in any direction." — Joel Watts
How to: Hold a kettlebell or dumbbell at chest level. Squat down keeping your chest up and knees tracking over toes. Drive through your heels to stand. This builds the power you need for quick direction changes.

🎯 Core Stability Exercises

6 Pallof Press

Benefits: "Requires you to display aspects of strength, balance, and core stability. You'll not only strengthen your core but also increase your mobility." — Andrew Blosser
How to: Stand sideways to a cable machine or resistance band anchored at chest height. Press the handle straight out in front of you, holding for 2-3 seconds, then return. This anti-rotation exercise builds stability for all pickleball movements.

7 Single-Arm Landmine Press

Benefits: "Beneficial for safely strengthening the shoulders while also improving the mobility of the shoulder joint. Your core stability will be challenged." — Andrew Blosser
How to: Hold one end of a barbell or landmine attachment at shoulder height. Press the weight up and slightly across your body, then lower with control. Alternate arms. Great for shoulder health and rotational power.

🏓 Pickleball-Specific Training Tips

  • 2-3 times per week — Allow recovery days between sessions
  • Focus on functional movements — Rotational exercises directly translate to shot power
  • Don't neglect single-leg exercises — Pickleball requires constant balance on one leg
  • Shoulder mobility matters — The overhead press and landmine press help maintain range of motion
  • Core stability is key — Every shot starts from a stable core
  • Start light, focus on form — Quality of movement trumps heavy weights
